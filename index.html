Experiment 1: Identifying Crystal Symmetries & Zone Axes

In Laue diffraction, when the incident X-ray beam is aligned parallel to a major crystallographic symmetry axis, the diffraction pattern displays the rotational symmetry of that axis (Friedel's law also adds an inversion center). For a cubic crystal:
• The [001] direction has 4-fold rotational symmetry (square spot layout).
• The [110] direction has 2-fold rotational symmetry (rhomb/mirror symmetry).
• The [111] direction has 3-fold rotational symmetry (hexagonal/trigonal arrangement).

Experiment 2: Duane-Hunt Limit & Measuring Planck's Constant

Experiment 3: Structure Factor Extinctions & Bravais Lattices

Constructive interference occurs only when the structure factor \(F(h,k,l) \ne 0\). Due to destructive phase cancellations between basis atoms, certain classes of reflections are strictly forbidden:

Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)
Rule: \(h, k, l\) must be all unmixed (all even or all odd).
Allowed: (111), (200), (220)
Forbidden: (100), (110), (210)
Body-Centered Cubic (BCC)
Rule: \(h + k + l\) must be even.
Allowed: (110), (200), (211)
Forbidden: (100), (111), (210)
Diamond Cubic (Si, C)
Rule: Unmixed, and if all even, \(h+k+l = 4n\).
Allowed: (111), (220), (311)
Forbidden: (200), (222)

Theoretical Principles of Laue X-Ray Diffraction

Mathematical formulation of reciprocal space, Ewald spheres, and conic section zone projections

1. Vector Formulation of Bragg's Law & Laue Condition

Let \(\hat{s}_0\) be the unit vector along the incident X-ray beam and \(\hat{s}\) be the unit vector along the diffracted ray. The scattering vector is \(\Delta \vec{k} = \vec{k}' - \vec{k} = \frac{\hat{s} - \hat{s}_0}{\lambda}\). The diffraction condition states that elastic scattering occurs when the scattering vector equals a reciprocal lattice vector \(\vec{G}_{hkl}\):

\(\hat{s} - \hat{s}_0 = \lambda \vec{G}_{hkl}\)

Taking the scalar square on both sides with \(|\hat{s}| = |\hat{s}_0| = 1\):

\(\lambda = \frac{-2(\hat{s}_0 \cdot \vec{G}_{hkl})}{|\vec{G}_{hkl}|^2}\)

In polychromatic (white beam) Laue diffraction, the wavelength is not fixed; rather, the crystal automatically selects the unique wavelength \(\lambda\) from the continuous spectrum that satisfies this condition!

2. Duane-Hunt Law & Continuous Bremsstrahlung Spectrum

Electrons hitting the anode target decelerate rapidly, converting their kinetic energy \(eV\) into X-ray photons. By conservation of energy, the maximum photon energy corresponds to the minimum wavelength:

\(E_{\max} = h \nu_{\max} = \frac{hc}{\lambda_{\min}} = eV \implies \lambda_{\min} = \frac{hc}{eV} \approx \frac{12.3984}{V \text{ (in kV)}} \text{ Å}\)

3. Geometric Origin of Zone Conic Sections

All crystal planes parallel to a zone axis \([u v w]\) satisfy \(u h + v k + w l = 0\). If the zone axis makes an angle \(\alpha\) with the incident beam, all diffracted rays form a right circular cone of semi-vertical angle \(\alpha\) coaxial with the zone axis. The intersection of this cone with a flat detector plate at distance \(D\) produces a conic section:

  • Transmission Laue: The forward-scattered cone intersects the plate in an ellipse passing through the direct beam center.
  • Back-Reflection Laue: The backward-scattered rays intersect the plate in a hyperbola branch.
